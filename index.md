<section id="about">
  <h2>About Me</h2>

  <p>
    I am an archaeologist and anthropologist whose work examines relationships
    among people, landscapes, climate, and environmental change through the Holocene.
  </p>

  <p>
    I am currently a Lecturer &amp; Research Associate in Archaeology and Co-Director
    of the Cultural Resources Facility at Cal Poly Humboldt. I lead archaeological
    investigations in California, write technical reports, train students in
    archaeological field methods, and collaborate with local, state, and federal
    agencies on cultural-resource management and National Register assessments.
  </p>

  <p>
    Before joining Cal Poly Humboldt, I was Project Director for the Kentucky
    Archaeological Survey at Western Kentucky University, where I led Phase I–III
    archaeological investigations throughout Kentucky.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="research">
  <h2>My Research</h2>

  <p>
    My research focuses on human and ecosystem responses to environmental change,
    particularly during the Middle Holocene Climatic Optimum approximately
    8,000–5,000 years ago and the role of Archaic hunter-gatherers in creating
    anthropogenic environments with fire between approximately 5,000 and 3,000
    years ago in Eastern North America.
  </p>

  <p>
    I use geoarchaeological methods derived from soil sciences—including magnetic
    susceptibility, loss-on-ignition analysis, and archaeological micromorphology—
    to study landform histories in relation to climatic change and Native lifeways
    throughout the Holocene.
  </p>

  <h3>Research interests</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Geoarchaeology and soil geomorphology</li>
    <li>Environmental and landscape archaeology</li>
    <li>Middle and Late Holocene climate change</li>
    <li>Archaic hunter-gatherers and mobility</li>
    <li>Anthropogenic fire and ecosystem management</li>
    <li>Native lifeways and human–environment relationships</li>
    <li>Archaeological site formation processes</li>
  </ul>
</section>
