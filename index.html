About Me

I am an archaeologist and anthropologist whose work examines relationships among people, landscapes, climate, and environmental change through the Holocene.

I am a Lecturer & Research Associate in Archaeology and Co-Director of the Cultural Resources Facility at Cal Poly Humboldt. My work includes archaeological investigations, technical reporting, student training in archaeological field methods, and cultural-resource management.

Before joining Cal Poly Humboldt, I served as Project Director for the Kentucky Archaeological Survey at Western Kentucky University, where I led archaeological investigations throughout Kentucky.

My Research

My research focuses on human and ecosystem responses to environmental change, particularly during the Middle Holocene, as well as the role of hunter-gatherers in shaping anthropogenic environments through fire in Eastern North America.

I use geoarchaeological methods derived from soil science, including magnetic susceptibility, loss-on-ignition analysis, and archaeological micromorphology, to study landform histories, climatic change, archaeological site formation, and Native lifeways throughout the Holocene.
